Root Cause & Resolution

This document presents a systematic validation of the GODMAX covariance pipeline, with the goal of identifying the origin of small negative eigenvalues observed in multi-probe Gaussian + non-Gaussian covariance matrices. The analysis proceeds from the 3-D halo-model power spectra through Limber projection and angular consistency checks, and finally to covariance construction and numerical stability diagnostics. A fully independent implementation of the Gaussian covariance using the Knox formula is included as a final cross-check. The Knox-based covariance is found to be identical to the GODMAX Gaussian covariance to floating-point precision, confirming the correctness of the implementation. The key conclusion is that all observed negative eigenvalues are at the $\sim 10^{-27}$ level and arise from floating-point cancellations, not from any physical inconsistency or error in the pipeline.

§4 - 3-D Power Spectra & Cauchy–Schwarz Consistency

Before investigating the covariance, we verify that the six 3-D power spectra $P_{xx}(k,z)$ from the halo model are self-consistent. The Cauchy–Schwarz condition in 3-D is

$$r_{xy} \equiv \frac{P_{xy}^2(k,z)}{P_{xx}(k,z)\,P_{yy}(k,z)} \leq 1$$

for all $(k,z)$. Violation would indicate inconsistent profile integrals before Limber projection.

§7 - Baryonic Suppression Factor Analysis

What $P_\mathrm{sup}$ is

The suppression factor is defined as $$S(k,z) \equiv \frac{P_\mathrm{halofit}^\mathrm{DM}(k,z)}{P_\mathrm{NFW,\,tot}(k,z)}$$ It is a halo-model accuracy correction (not a baryonic correction): it absorbs the well-known 10–20% inaccuracy of the NFW halo model relative to $N$-body results.

Spectrum$P_\mathrm{sup}$ applied?Physical justification
$P_{mm}$× S(k,z)DM halo model correction - exact calibration target
$P_{gm}$, $P_{gg}$× S(k,z)Approximate - $S$ not calibrated for biased tracers
$P_{ym}$, $P_{gy}$, $P_{yy}$× 1 (none)Pressure profile fully baryonified (BCMP → $P_e$ → $y_\mathrm{3D}$); no NFW → no correction needed
Note on the pressure profile The tSZ chain is $(\theta_\mathrm{ej}, \theta_\mathrm{co}, \beta) \to \rho_\mathrm{gas} \to T_\mathrm{gas}\ [\text{HSE} + f_\mathrm{nt}] \to P_e \to y_\mathrm{3D}$. There is no NFW component anywhere in this chain, so $P_\mathrm{sup}$ is physically inapplicable to $P_{yy}$.

§8 - Fix A: Remove $P_\mathrm{sup}$ from $P_{gg}$

Motivation. $S(k,z)$ is calibrated against the matter power spectrum, not biased HOD tracers. Removing it from $P_{gg}$ restores the natural C-S condition $C_{gy}^2 \leq C_{gg} \cdot C_{yy}$ without making any unphysical assumptions about the tSZ field.

§9 - Fix B: Apply $P_\mathrm{sup}$ Consistently to $P_{gy}$ and $P_{yy}$

Motivation. Keep the $P_{gg}$ suppression but also suppress $P_{gy}$ and $P_{yy}$ by $S$ so that C-S is restored:

$$(P_{gy} \cdot S)^2 \leq (P_{gg} \cdot S)\cdot(P_{yy} \cdot S) \iff P_{gy}^2 \leq P_{gg}\cdot P_{yy}$$

which holds naturally from the halo model.

Multi-bin Schur complement

With $N_\mathrm{lens} = 5$ lens bins, the relevant PSD condition is the Schur complement of the full $(N_\mathrm{lens}+1)$ Cl matrix: $$\mathcal{S}(\ell) = C_{yy}(\ell) - \mathbf{C}_{gy}^T(\ell)\,\mathbf{C}_{gg}^{-1}(\ell)\,\mathbf{C}_{gy}(\ell) \geq 0$$ This is strictly tighter than the per-bin condition $C_{gy,b}^2 \leq C_{gg,bb} \cdot C_{yy}$ because the cross-bin galaxy correlations $C_{gg,b_1 b_2}$ couple all lens bins simultaneously.

Fig 8b. Schur complement $\mathcal{S}(\ell)$ for default, Fix A, Fix B. Fix A keeps $\mathcal{S} > 0$ at all ells. Fix B marginally violates it at the same ells where the raw spectra have the 3-D C-S issue, since lowering $C_{yy}$ tightens the condition.

Root cause

The full validation pipeline demonstrates that the GODMAX covariance implementation is internally consistent and numerically correct. The independent Knox-formula reconstruction reproduces the Gaussian covariance exactly, ruling out any implementation error in covG_dict. The only physically identifiable inconsistency is the asymmetric application of $P_\mathrm{sup}$, which produces a small 3-D and angular Cauchy–Schwarz violation. However, this effect does not drive the covariance eigenvalue behaviour. The observed negative eigenvalues arise entirely from floating-point cancellations at the $10^{-27}$ level and are therefore numerically insignificant.

Recommended fixes

Immediate (numerical): regularize with eigenvalue clipping. 
def regularize_cov(cov, floor=0.0):
    ev, V = np.linalg.eigh(cov)
    ratio = abs(ev.min()) / ev.max()
    print(f"|λ_min|/λ_max = {ratio:.2e}  (safe to regularize if < 1e-10)")
    return V @ np.diag(np.maximum(ev, floor)) @ V.T

§12 - Impact of PSD Clipping on the Covariance

Given that the negative eigenvalues in the covariance matrix are purely numerical (at the $\sim 10^{-27}$ level), we test whether enforcing positive semi-definiteness (PSD) through eigenvalue clipping produces any physically meaningful change in the covariance.

The procedure is:

  • Diagonalize the covariance matrix: $C = V \Lambda V^\top$
  • Set all negative eigenvalues to zero: $\Lambda \rightarrow \max(\Lambda, 0)$
  • Reconstruct the clipped covariance: $C_{\mathrm{clipped}} = V \Lambda V^\top$

This isolates the impact of numerical PSD violations while preserving the eigenstructure of the covariance.
